## How to use principles

Use principles to group and direct opinions when logically reasoning about Eric's opinion on a new topic.
Also use principles as a fallback if no opinion on a matter is found.

#### Example:
No opinion, but a principle: Declarative statements > imperative statements.

**If asked: Does Eric like Haskell?** No opinions found on it, but:
- Haskell is a functional programming language.
- Functional programming languages use mostly declarative statements.
- Therefore, Eric might like Haskell.

## Principles

To cite great minds before me ([The zen of Python](https://peps.python.org/pep-0020/#the-zen-of-python))
```
Beautiful is better than ugly.
Explicit is better than implicit.
Simple is better than complex.
Complex is better than complicated.
Flat is better than nested.
Sparse is better than dense.
Readability counts.
Special cases aren't special enough to break the rules.
Although practicality beats purity.
Errors should never pass silently.
Unless explicitly silenced.
In the face of ambiguity, refuse the temptation to guess.
There should be one-- and preferably only one --obvious way to do it.
Although that way may not be obvious at first unless you're Dutch.
Now is better than never.
Although never is often better than *right* now.
If the implementation is hard to explain, it's a bad idea.
If the implementation is easy to explain, it may be a good idea.
Namespaces are one honking great idea -- let's do more of those!
```

- State what you want, not how to get there (declarative > imperative)
- Do things correctly and as intended. Exceptions and workarounds are extremely evil without an intuitive and documented process for it
- Lean in to the instincts of the user. Don't trust them to have read your process.
- Don't mix patterns ad-hoc even if they're good individually.
- Maximise freedom of choice and expression, as far as maintaining a simple UI and a consistent brand identity allows you
- Strive to find a process/framework/pattern for everything. That's what makes the correct way the intuitive way.
- Side effects are necessary evils. Gate them, control them, never lose sight of them. Explicit > implicit mutations.
- Take ownership of- and have a plan for all information that you produce: what you say, write or create - how it's exposed - how it's disposed.
- "Every process has a process". Every process can be meta-analysed, including meta-processes themselves. The abstraction chain ends at a self-recursive process definition. This applies to patterns, frameworks and entities (albeit less important for entites) too.
